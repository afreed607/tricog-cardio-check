# Diagnostic centres

Diagnostic centres handle volume and are measured on turnaround and consistency. TCC adds a cardiac screening layer at registration, identifying which attendees warrant a 12-lead ECG before they leave the building.

## The problem in this setting

Patients arrive for a specific ordered test. Cardiac risk outside that order goes unexamined, even though the patient is physically present and a screening opportunity exists that will not recur.

## The constraints

- **Tests are performed to order**, not to clinical judgement.
- **Throughput pressure** and fixed appointment slots.
- **Interpretation capacity is the bottleneck**, not acquisition.
- **Consistency across branches and shifts** is what the centre is judged on.

## How cardiac risk is handled today

Tests performed as ordered, with cardiac assessment only where explicitly requested by the referring clinician.

## What TCC changes

TCC screens at registration during vitals. Where a result is Moderate or High, the centre can offer a 12-lead ECG in the same visit and route it through InstaECG for AI-assisted, specialist-verified reporting within minutes, rather than asking the patient to return.

In the wider care network, primary care health centres and diagnostic centres act as catchment areas, screening locally and referring patients with abnormalities to tertiary hubs for further diagnostics.

## What it delivers

- **Cardiac screening added without a new appointment type.**
- **Same-visit escalation** from screening to diagnostic ECG.
- **A uniform standard across branches**, applied by the same model regardless of location.
- **Appropriate 12-lead volume** from patients who would otherwise have left unscreened.
