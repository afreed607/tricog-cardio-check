# GP clinics

**Summary.** A single-doctor practice with no diagnostics on site needs a structured way to decide who to refer onward. TCC supplies a consistent framework rather than leaving it to individual judgement under time pressure.

## The problem

A GP seeing patients back-to-back has limited time and incomplete information, and no on-site test to resolve a cardiac question. The decision to refer rests entirely on presentation and history.

## Challenges

- No on-site ECG interpretation, and often no ECG machine
- No colleague to consult on an unfamiliar trace
- Referral thresholds vary between practitioners and across a single practitioner's day
- An unfamiliar ECG pattern at an inconvenient hour is a known failure point

## How this is handled today

Referral on clinical suspicion, or referral for a 12-lead at another facility with results returning days later.

## The TCC approach

TCC gives the practice a consistent, clinically grounded starting point: a Low, Moderate or High category on every patient, applied the same way regardless of who is on duty or how busy the session is. The framework works identically in a metro practice and a small-town one.

## Outcomes

- Consistent referral thresholds across practitioners and sessions
- A defensible, documented reason for each referral
- Reassurance for Low-risk patients delivered during the same visit

## Evidence

Low-risk sensitivity 90.2% (95% CI 89.2–91.2%) supports safely routing stable patients out of the cardiac pathway. See [clinical validation](../clinical-validation.md).
