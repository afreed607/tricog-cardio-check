# Clinical and operational value

Cardiac care in a high-volume facility faces a dual challenge. Clinically, physicians confront the asymptomatic patient — someone who appears stable but harbours early-stage abnormality that routine assessment misses. Operationally, patient volumes grow while the availability of specialists and care teams stays static. Running a 12-lead ECG on every walk-in would address the clinical gap by making the operational one worse.

TCC addresses both at once, and the value it creates differs by who is looking at it.

## The operational problems it addresses

**The triage and prioritisation crisis.** Without instant diagnostic clarity, overcrowded facilities default to a linear, first-come-first-served queue rather than prioritising by verified clinical risk. This compromises acute cardiac patients by delaying intervention and raises the likelihood of missing the Golden Hour for acute ischaemic events.

**The diagnostic bottleneck.** Deploying portable hardware is only half a solution. Deciphering raw waveforms demands specialised cardiological expertise, and relying on manual human review of every incoming screening trace creates a data bottleneck that reduces throughput and negates the speed advantage of a portable device.

**Specialist bandwidth exhaustion.** When frontline staff have no automated stratification tool, physicians absorb the diagnostic load and find themselves evaluating large volumes of benign or low-risk data. That misallocation depletes the clinical time and mental bandwidth available for the complex, high-risk cases that genuinely require specialist expertise.

## For the physician

A noise-free workflow. Acting as a clinical microscope, TCC highlights minute, sub-clinical markers, allowing physicians to catch silent disease in asymptomatic patients before it becomes an emergency. Acting as a sieve, it ensures specialists see the sickest patients first, preserving their attention for complex cases.

It also changes what a referral decision rests on. Traditionally a physician recommends a 12-lead ECG based on symptoms and history, which means patients who need the test sometimes do not receive it, and patients who do not need it sometimes do. Screening every patient with a single-lead ECG lets physicians identify who actually requires the more complex test.

## For frontline and ground staff

TCC translates complex waveforms into a clear Red / Orange / Green risk signal. That output lets triage nurses and frontline staff manage patient flow confidently, segregate queues safely and escalate critical cases immediately, without waiting for a specialist interpretation and without needing to read the trace themselves.

Because the model is additive only, a staff member acting on a TCC result cannot make triage worse than it would have been without one.

## For the institution

**Maximised resource utilisation.** Cardiologists' and physicians' time is the scarcest resource in the facility. Filtering patients by risk level means the patients with Moderate or High risk are prioritised, and specialist attention and confirmatory testing are directed to the patients who need them — with no added staff.

**Increased throughput and reduced waiting.** Stratifying risk at the point of care resolves the operational bottleneck: critical interventions wait less, and low-risk patients move through standard flow without consuming advanced capacity.

**A consistent standard across locations.** The same classification criteria are applied by the same model at every site, regardless of who is on duty. For administration and medical superintendents this is a standard-of-care argument: it reduces missed early-risk cases facility-wide and across a network.

**Revenue from appropriate testing.** Identifying more patients who genuinely warrant a 12-lead ECG increases appropriate test volume, and patients found to have a cardiac condition may go on to treatment at the same centre. For hospitals and clinics with on-site diagnostic capability, that testing and any subsequent treatment is revenue that would not have been generated, because those patients would never have been flagged.

**Improved patient care and outcomes.** Screening every patient who enters means patients who would have been overlooked get the opportunity for further evaluation and entry into the care pathway. Equally, filtering out low-risk patients makes the diagnostic process more efficient, so the cardiac pathway does not become a conveyor belt that sends everyone for advanced testing.

## For the patient

A patient arriving for an unrelated complaint receives a cardiac risk assessment they did not ask for and would not otherwise have had, at no additional appointment and at the cost of about a minute. If the result is Low, they continue their visit and still see the doctor. If it is Moderate or High, they enter a care pathway while still in the building, rather than discovering the problem after an acute event.

For asymptomatic patients specifically — who by definition have no reason to seek cardiac assessment — this is often the only screening touchpoint they will encounter.

## Where the value is greatest

TCC is most effective as a mass screening tool in OPD and medical camp settings, where rapid assessment of large patient volumes is the requirement. It is also gaining use inside doctors' chambers as a point-of-care aid during consultation.

It delivers most where clinical workflows are not clearly established, since the structured Low / Moderate / High output imposes a consistent process where none existed. And its portability suits rural and geographically dispersed areas, where the only physical requirement is the OMRON Complete device.
