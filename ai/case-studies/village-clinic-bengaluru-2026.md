# Village clinic, Bengaluru — 400+ screened in a single day

**Summary.** On 8 June 2026, Tricog CardioCheck ran its first large-scale field deployment at a village clinic on the outskirts of Bengaluru founded by Dr. Ramana Rao. Six OMRON Complete devices screened 400+ patients in a single day, delivering a complete care journey — TCC, then 12-lead ECG, then the doctor.

## Customer profile

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| Setting | Village clinic, outskirts of Bengaluru |
| Founded by | Dr. Ramana Rao |
| Operating pattern | Every Sunday for over five decades |
| Population served | Hundreds of patients who may otherwise have had no access to care |
| Existing cardiac infrastructure | None on site |

## Initial challenge

A weekly clinic serving hundreds of patients with no on-site cardiac screening capability. Patients arrived for general care; cardiac risk went unexamined unless it presented obviously. The clinic had reach and trust but no way to identify who among those hundreds carried cardiac risk.

## Intervention

- **Six OMRON Complete devices** screened patients waiting outside the clinic, with a dedicated station inside ensuring no one was missed.
- **Two volunteers per screening point.** One guided patient posture, collected details and explained the result; the other logged data and coordinated next steps.
- **Referral onward.** Patients identified as Moderate or High risk were referred for a 12-lead ECG and further care.
- **Clinical decisions stayed with the treating doctors throughout.** TCC's job was to sort the queue and surface who needed attention first, not to diagnose.

## Results

- **400+ patients screened in a single day.**
- A complete care journey delivered in the field: TCC, then 12-lead ECG, then clinical consult.
- Field validation of device performance, app sync and workflow.
- Rich feedback from patients, doctors and stakeholders on usability and experience.

## Metrics

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| Patients screened | 400+ |
| Duration | 1 day |
| Devices deployed | 6 |
| Staff per screening point | 2 volunteers |
| Specialist operators required | 0 |
| Screening stations | Multiple outdoor + 1 dedicated indoor |

## Observations

Several patients expressed relief after a Low-risk result and encouraged family members to get screened. Others approached out of curiosity, wanting to understand how the device worked and what it could tell them. The clinic's own team, screening themselves, gave encouraging feedback on both the platform and the presence of volunteers.

## Lessons learned

- **Non-specialist volunteers can operate the capture reliably at scale.** No cardiologist was present, and 400+ screenings completed in a day.
- **A two-person station works.** Separating patient guidance from data logging kept throughput up without compromising either.
- **Screening the waiting area, not a room, is what produces the volume.** The dedicated indoor station existed to catch anyone the outdoor points missed, not as the primary channel.
- **The result has to be explained on the spot.** Patients acted on Low results socially — telling family to get screened — which only happens if someone explains what the category means.
- **Completing the journey matters.** Referring to a 12-lead and a doctor in the same visit is what turned screening into care.

## Limitations

This was a single-day deployment in one setting, reported by the company. Downstream diagnostic outcomes for referred patients are not published, so the case evidences operational feasibility rather than clinical yield. See [research gaps](../research/index.md).
