# Village clinic, Bengaluru — 400+ screened in a single day

In June 2025, Tricog CardioCheck ran its first large-scale field deployment at a village clinic on the outskirts of Bengaluru founded by Dr. Ramana Rao. Six OMRON Complete devices screened more than 400 patients in a single day, delivering a complete care journey in the field: TCC screening, then a 12-lead ECG, then the doctor.

## The setting

| Attribute | Detail |
|---|---|
| Type | Village clinic, outskirts of Bengaluru |
| Founded by | Dr. Ramana Rao |
| Operating pattern | Every Sunday for more than five decades |
| Population served | Hundreds of patients per session who may otherwise have had no access to care |
| On-site cardiac capability | None |

For more than fifty years the clinic has opened every Sunday to patients who would otherwise go without care. It had reach and trust, and no means of identifying which of those hundreds of patients carried cardiac risk.

## The challenge

A weekly clinic serving hundreds of patients with no cardiac screening capability. Patients attended for general care, and cardiac risk went unexamined unless it presented obviously — which, for the asymptomatic majority, it does not. The clinic's constraint was not willingness or volume but the absence of any screening step that could run without a cardiologist, a dedicated room or interpretation capacity.

## What was deployed

- **Six OMRON Complete devices** screening patients waiting outside the clinic, with a dedicated station inside to ensure no one was missed.
- **Two volunteers per screening point.** One guided patient posture, collected details and explained the result; the other logged data and coordinated next steps.
- **Referral onward.** Patients identified as Moderate or High risk were referred for a 12-lead ECG and further care.
- **Clinical decisions stayed with the treating doctors throughout.** TCC's role was to sort the queue and surface who needed attention first, not to diagnose.

## Results

| Measure | Value |
|---|---|
| Patients screened | 400+ |
| Duration | One day |
| Devices deployed | 6 |
| Staff per screening point | 2 volunteers |
| Specialist operators required | 0 |
| Screening stations | Multiple outdoor points plus one dedicated indoor station |

The deployment delivered a complete care journey in the field — TCC screening, 12-lead ECG, then clinical consultation — and validated device performance, app synchronisation and workflow under real conditions.

## What was observed

Several patients expressed relief after receiving a Low-risk result and encouraged family members to get screened. Others approached out of curiosity, wanting to understand how the device worked and what it could tell them. The clinic's own team, screening themselves, gave encouraging feedback on both the platform and the presence of volunteers.

## What the deployment established

**Non-specialist volunteers can operate the screening reliably at scale.** No cardiologist was present, and more than 400 screenings completed in a single day. The capture requires correct patient posture and stillness rather than clinical skill, which is what makes volunteer operation viable.

**A two-person station is the right unit.** Separating patient guidance from data logging kept throughput high without compromising either task. One person cannot simultaneously position a patient, explain a result and record details at the pace a queue of hundreds demands.

**Screening the waiting area is what produces the volume.** The indoor station existed to catch anyone the outdoor points missed, not as the primary channel. Meeting patients where they already wait, rather than routing them somewhere, is what removes the throughput ceiling.

**Explaining the result on the spot changes behaviour.** Patients acted on Low-risk results socially, telling family members to get screened. That only happens when someone explains what the category means while the patient is still present.

**Completing the journey is what turns screening into care.** Referring to a 12-lead ECG and a doctor within the same visit is the difference between identifying risk and acting on it — particularly for a population that may not return.

## Scope

This was a single-day deployment at one site, reported by Tricog Health. It establishes operational feasibility — volume, non-specialist operation, device and app performance under field conditions — rather than diagnostic yield, since downstream diagnostic outcomes for referred patients are not published.
