# The Tricog Connected Pathway

Tricog CardioCheck is the first decision in a wider cardiac ecosystem, where the result of one step is available in time, and in usable form, to guide the next. Five stages, and patients leave at any of them.

| Stage | Product | The question it answers |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Screen | **Tricog CardioCheck** | Who may need a diagnostic ECG? |
| 2. Diagnose | **InstaECG** | What does the 12-lead show, read by a specialist, within minutes? |
| 3. Detect hidden risk | **Tricog LVEF algorithm** | Could this patient also have reduced cardiac function? |
| 4. Confirm | **InstaEcho** | Does the echo confirm a structural or functional problem? |
| 5. Follow | **KeeboHealth** | What changes after the patient goes home? |

**Cardionet** runs alongside, coordinating referring centre, cardiologist, ambulance team and receiving hospital so a critical alert becomes an actual transfer of care — as in the STEMI Goa Program.

## Where the pathway starts

Digital health programmes often begin with a device and then look for somewhere to use it. The Connected Pathway begins instead with the points at which cardiac care slows down:

- the patient is not identified early
- the ECG is acquired but not interpreted in time
- heart failure risk remains hidden
- an echo is delayed
- follow-up becomes episodic after discharge

Each product addresses one of these, but the value appears when the output of one step leads directly to the next action rather than into a queue.

## Three patients, one door

Three people sit a few chairs apart in the same OPD. One has come for a routine follow-up and mentions occasional discomfort only when the nurse probes. One reports chest heaviness but is speaking normally and does not appear acutely ill. One has known hypertension and diabetes and has recently become more breathless doing ordinary housework.

They entered through the same door. They do not have to wait in the same order or undergo the same tests.

- The first screens Low. With a reassuring clinical assessment, they return to the usual OPD process.
- The second produces a critical signal and is brought forward for urgent medical review.
- The third is classified as carrying risk — stable, but with reason to investigate rather than a vague instruction to return if symptoms worsen. That patient continues through a 12-lead ECG, an LVEF risk assessment and an echo before treatment and post-discharge monitoring.

The value of the pathway lies in these being three different outcomes, because a care system works well when it knows both whom to move forward and whom not to.

## Patients leave at every stage

A well-designed pathway removes people at every stage where further cardiac investigation is not needed, and creates a fast route around the usual sequence when a critical finding demands immediate action.

"Connected" does not mean more tests for everyone. A normal 12-lead does not cancel symptoms or replace a doctor's judgement, but it does prevent the cardiac pathway becoming a conveyor belt that sends every patient for advanced testing. The pathway is as much about confidently routing people out as about moving people through.

## How it behaves by setting

**In a tertiary hospital**, the pressure is volume. Capacity — cardiologists, echo, cath lab — exists but is shared across many patients. TCC creates order at the front of the OPD. InstaECG and InstaEcho reduce reporting delays. The LVEF algorithm adds a signal without an additional test. Critical cases move faster, while normal and lower-risk patients do not consume advanced capacity unnecessarily. KeeboHealth keeps selected patients on organised follow-up after discharge.

**In a smaller clinic or health centre**, the first requirement is recognising risk with limited local infrastructure. TCC supports that first decision. If a 12-lead can be acquired on site, InstaECG connects the clinic to remote interpretation; if it cannot, the screening result supports a referral. The LVEF assessment identifies which abnormal ECGs need an echo, and InstaEcho supports reporting where image acquisition is available. When advanced treatment is required, the patient is referred onward with a clearer clinical picture and a digital trail of what has already been done.

Expanding access to cardiac care does not require every facility to have every capability. It requires each facility to recognise what it can do safely, connect to the next level when required, and avoid losing the patient in between. Primary care health centres and diagnostic centres function as catchment areas in this model, screening locally and referring abnormalities to tertiary hubs.

## Beyond treatment

Hospitals are generally well organised around the visible episodes of care: admission, procedure, observation, discharge. The harder period begins after the patient goes home, where instructions that were clear in the ward become harder to follow. Weight may rise, blood pressure may drift outside range, symptoms may return, medicines may be missed, or a rhythm problem may appear between scheduled visits — and the next appointment could be weeks away.

A pathway that ends at discharge ends precisely when the hospital's view of the patient is weakest. KeeboHealth extends it, so a relevant change becomes visible early enough for a clinician to decide what, if anything, should be done.
