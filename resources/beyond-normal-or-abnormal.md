# Beyond "normal" or "abnormal": every ECG as a clear call to action

An ECG can save a life but only if the right person sees it at the right time, and knows exactly how urgently to act. In busy hospitals, clinics, and primary care setups, thousands of ECGs are recorded every day, and not every reading gets the same level of attention it deserves. A subtle ST-segment change buried among routine traces can be just as consequential as an obvious emergency if no one is looking for it in time.

This is the exact problem Tricog’s TCC (Tricog Cardiac Care) risk stratification framework was built to solve. Rather than treating every ECG as either “normal” or “needs a cardiologist,” TCC sorts findings into three clinically meaningful tiers Low, Moderate, and High risk so that care teams know at a glance not just what an ECG shows, but how fast they need to move.

Why risk stratification matters more than diagnosis alone

A diagnosis tells you what’s happening in the heart. Risk stratification tells you what to do next.

For a referring physician juggling dozens of patients, or a technician in a remote clinic without an on-site cardiologist, that distinction is everything. TCC’s framework gives every ECG a triage signal a shared language that helps hospitals, diagnostic centres, and referring doctors act with the same urgency a specialist would apply, even when a specialist isn’t in the room.

The three tiers, and what they mean in practice

Low risk covers ECGs that are normal or reflect well-recognised benign variants a Normal Sinus Rhythm, Sinus Arrhythmia, or Benign Early Repolarisation, for example. These findings carry no evidence of structural, ischaemic, conduction, or rhythm pathology. Nothing urgent needs to happen here beyond routine follow-up, which matters just as much as flagging danger: it keeps care teams from chasing false alarms and lets them focus attention where it’s genuinely needed.

Moderate risk is where clinical judgement earns its keep. This tier includes findings such as old infarctions, suspected ischaemia, complete bundle branch block, atrial enlargement or inter-atrial block, and ventricular hypertrophy real abnormalities that point to underlying cardiovascular disease, but not an active emergency. These cases don’t need a blue-light response; they need a plan. TCC flags them for further evaluation, investigation, or monitoring, so they don’t fall through the cracks between “fine” and “critical.”

High risk is the tier built for speed. New-onset or haemodynamically significant atrial fibrillation or flutter, life-threatening arrhythmias, critical conduction disorders, and acute ischaemic patterns all fall here findings associated with a real risk of haemodynamic compromise, cardiac arrest, or acute ischaemic injury. TCC’s framework is designed to surface these instantly, so escalation and intervention begin without the delay that a busy queue or an unfamiliar reader might otherwise introduce.

Where this makes the biggest difference

The value of this framework shows up most clearly in the moments care teams face every day: a general physician staring at an unfamiliar ECG pattern at 2 a.m., a technician in a Tier-3 town without a cardiologist on call, or a hospital trying to standardise how its emergency department triages chest pain. In each case, TCC’s Low/Moderate/High structure converts a raw trace into a decision assess now, follow up soon, or reassure and move on backed by consistent, clinically grounded criteria rather than individual judgement alone.

That consistency is what makes TCC useful at scale. A framework that works the same way whether it’s applied in a metro hospital or a rural clinic is a framework that closes the gap between where a cardiac emergency happens and where expert cardiac care actually lives.

### Key Takeways

Three tiers Low, Moderate and High turn every ECG into a triage decision, not just a diagnosis.

Low risk: normal traces and well-recognised benign variants; routine follow-up only.

Moderate risk: real pathology without an active emergency flagged for evaluation, investigation or monitoring.

High risk: life-threatening arrhythmias, critical conduction disorders and acute ischaemia surfaced instantly for escalation.

Judgement first, always

It’s worth being clear about what this framework is not. TCC risk stratification is a triage aid, not a replacement for the treating physician. Every category is meant to be interpreted alongside the patient’s clinical presentation, and confirmed through proper diagnostic evaluation with a 12-lead ECG. What it does is give clinicians a faster, more consistent starting point so the right cases get urgent eyes on them immediately, and the right resources aren’t spent chasing the ones that don’t need them.

In cardiac care, the cost of ambiguity is measured in minutes. Tricog’s TCC framework exists to strip that ambiguity out of the first, most critical step: knowing how serious an ECG really is, the moment it’s recorded.
